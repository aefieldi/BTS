06
Why it's built this way

More friction, not less

I stopped inviting people into Skool from the DM.

That sounds backwards for someone closing her doors in twelve days. It isn't. I feel called to work with established entrepreneurs who already create content and already have momentum. So I'm qualifying them as early as I can: bio, then DM, then the free gift, then the audit. Every stage says the same thing: if you have this, this and this, book a call.

01 · Evergreen

Doors close October 7. I don't know when they reopen, at what price, or with what inside. An automation that says "join Skool" breaks the day they close. One that hands over a free gift and an audit link runs for a year.

  • Nothing in the DM has a date in it
  • The free gift page is the thing I update, not twelve automations
  • The audit is bookable by people who have never been in here

02 · Friction

Every stage names who it's for. Established platform, proven client results, already creating. If that's not you yet, you still get all the free stuff. You just don't get invited to a call you'd feel behind on.

  • Bio CTA: book your audit
  • DM: "if you're an established creator with proven client results"
  • Free gift: audit link top and bottom, the qualifier above it

03 · Trust

The free gift is every free training and guide I've ever made, on one page. Video trainings, cheat sheets, the 4 Stages of Ads, Human Design and astrology for business. Someone who works through even a third of it knows exactly how I think before they book.

  • Thirty-plus gems, nothing gated
  • The audit sells itself off the back of it
  • Or it doesn't, and they got thirty free things. Fine.

Traffic buys the follow. Engagement buys the comment. ManyChat asks for the follow again before it hands over the link. Two campaigns, one automation, and the audience compounds.

The number. $14 a day is $420 a month. Last month Instagram was 33% of all signups, on an account that was at zero followers thirty days ago. I'm not telling you that's the flex. I'm telling you the mechanism is cheap enough that there's no reason not to run it.
